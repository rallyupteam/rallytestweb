the challenge

a partner at an early-stage fund has a decade of opinions from thousands of pitches, and almost none of it was on linkedin. in the two months before rallyup, his posts reached a total of 32 impressions.

for an investor that's a deal-flow problem. founders pitch the investors they already know from their feed, and they check how an investor thinks before they take the meeting.

the solution

rallyup built the context first: how lightbank picks deals, the questions he asks founders, the patterns he sees across the portfolio, and the views he's willing to defend. then a paired human writer turned that into posts, every one approved in slack before it went live.

✓

the thesis, not the announcements. posts explain how he evaluates a pitch, what he asks founders for in their updates, and how fund incentives shape which companies get funded.

✓

useful to founders. the posts that travelled were the ones a founder could act on: a pitch structure, an update template, the four things an investment committee needs to hear.

✓

portfolio news, with a reason to read. funding announcements open with the problem the company solves, so they read as a story, not a press release.

the results

in two months, a near-silent profile became one founders save, share and argue with. reach went from 32 impressions to over 405,000, and 1,100 new people followed.
