linkedin ghostwriter vs agency: which should an executive hire?

a plain decision guide for founders and executives: what you actually buy with each, and how to choose.

published november 2026 · by francisco gost scagliarini, partner · 7 min read

last updated: november 2026

the short answer

Hire a freelance LinkedIn ghostwriter if you are a single executive with a modest budget and the time to brief, review and manage one writer yourself. Hire an agency if you want a fixed cadence, approvals, reporting, backup writers and distribution handled for you, or if more than one executive needs to post. rallyup is the strongest fit for B2B founders and executives who want a dedicated writer plus everything around the writing done for them; a freelancer or editor is better if you'd rather write your own posts and only need polish.

rallyup publishes this guide and is one of the options discussed. We are an agency, so read the sections on when a freelancer wins with that in mind.

the real difference: who runs the process

The writing itself is not what separates the two. A strong freelancer can write as well as anyone at an agency. What differs is everything that has to happen around the writing for LinkedIn to produce business results: deciding what to post about, getting material out of you, scheduling, keeping the profile in shape, growing the right audience, following up and measuring.

With a freelancer, most of that sits with you. With an agency, most of it sits with the agency. So the question to answer first is whether you, or someone on your team, will own that work every week for the next six months. If yes, a freelancer can be the efficient choice. If the honest answer is no, a freelancer usually turns into a few good months followed by drift.

when a freelancer wins

  • You are one executive and you enjoy the process. If you like choosing topics and reviewing drafts closely, a freelancer gives you a direct line to the person writing.
  • You already write and want an editor. If you produce rough drafts and need them tightened, an editor or freelance ghostwriter is the efficient buy. An agency would charge you for writing you don't need.
  • Your budget is under $5,000 a month. Many agency programs, ours included, start at that level per executive.
  • You have someone in-house to run distribution. If a marketer already handles your profile, scheduling, outreach and reporting, the agency extras overlap with work you already have covered.
  • You want to test whether LinkedIn suits you. A short freelance engagement is a lower-commitment way to find out if you like publishing.

when an agency wins

  • No one on your side will run the process. In our experience the most common way an executive LinkedIn program fades is that posting stops. A managed cadence guards against that.
  • You need it to drive pipeline, not just posts. Turning posts into conversations needs a profile that converts, an audience of buyers and follow-up. An agency that bundles those saves you stitching them together. For what that looks like when it's missing, see linkedin posts getting views but no leads.
  • More than one executive is posting. Coordinating topics and approvals across a founder, a CEO and a sales leader is a management job. At PandaDoc, three executives produced 1,235 engagements in one month under one program (PandaDoc case study).
  • You can't afford gaps. If a writer gets sick or leaves, an agency has someone who already knows your voice.
  • You want accountability in numbers. Monthly reporting and, in rallyup's case, a guarantee of 100k organic impressions in 90 days or you don't pay (for executives with $5m+ in ARR or funding).

questions to ask either one

Whichever route you take, these questions separate good options from risky ones:

  • Who exactly writes my posts, and will that stay the same person? Voice depends on continuity.
  • How do you get material from me? Look for regular interviews. A single questionnaire can run dry quickly.
  • Do you use AI to write? rallyup uses human writers and keeps AI to research. Whatever the answer, it should be clear.
  • How do approvals work? You should be able to approve, edit or reject every post before it goes live.
  • What do you report, and how often? Ask for a sample report. Impressions alone are not enough; look for profile views, ICP connections and inbound messages.
  • Can I see results for someone like me? Ask for named companies and real numbers. Ours are on our case studies page.

when rallyup isn't the right fit

  • Your budget is under $5,000 a month per executive.
  • You want to write it yourself and just need editing.
  • You need meetings in the next 30 days rather than an audience that produces them over a quarter.
  • Your goals are B2C or creator growth. We write for B2B founders and executives selling to other businesses.
  • You can't give a 30-minute story interview twice a month. That conversation is where the posts come from.

frequently asked questions

should an executive hire a linkedin ghostwriter or an agency?

Hire a freelance ghostwriter if you are one executive with a smaller budget and time to brief, review and manage one person. Hire an agency if you need a reliable cadence, approvals, reporting, backup writers and distribution, or if more than one executive will post. The deciding factor is usually who manages the work: you, or someone else.

is a freelance linkedin ghostwriter cheaper than an agency?

Usually, yes, on the monthly invoice. The difference is partly paid back in your own time, because with a freelancer you handle strategy, scheduling, profile changes, outreach and measurement yourself or not at all. Published entry prices for executive agency programs run from £1,500 a month (Kurogo) to $5,000 a month (Sculpt and rallyup).

what does a linkedin ghostwriting agency do that a freelancer doesn't?

An agency typically adds a managed process around the writing: a set interview rhythm, an approval workflow, publishing, reporting, cover when a writer is away and coordination across several executives. Some, including rallyup, also handle profile rewrites and ICP connection requests. A good freelancer can match the writing itself.

will an agency post sound less like me than a freelancer?

Not necessarily. Voice depends on how much time the writer spends with you and whether you keep the same writer. Ask any agency whether you get one dedicated writer and how often they interview you. rallyup assigns a dedicated human writer per executive and runs a story interview twice a month.

can i start with a freelancer and move to an agency later?

Yes, and many executives do. Keep your drafts, your best-performing posts and any notes on your voice, so the next writer starts with material instead of from zero.

how much of an executive's time does linkedin ghostwriting take?

With rallyup it is about 25 minutes for the onboarding form, a 30-minute session on the first drafts, a story interview twice a month and a few minutes a week approving posts in Slack. With a freelancer, add the time you spend managing the process yourself.
