what "end to end" should actually include

Many services call themselves done-for-you and then hand you drafts. Writing is one step of eight. If any of the others sits with you, the work is shared, and in our experience the part that sits with a busy founder is the part that stops first.

  • Strategy. Who the posts are for, what you want to be known for, and a weekly plan of topics.
  • Interviews. A set rhythm of conversations that pulls stories and opinions out of you, so posts don't run dry after a month.
  • Writing. Posts in your voice, by a writer who stays with you.
  • Approvals. A simple way to approve, edit or reject every post before it goes live.
  • Publishing. Someone schedules and posts for you, at the times your audience is online.
  • Profile. An About section and Featured links that turn a curious visitor into a conversation.
  • Audience. Connection requests to the buyers you want reading, so reach grows among the right people.
  • Reporting. A monthly view of impressions, followers, profile views, connections and inbound messages.

how to vet a done-for-you service

  • Walk the eight steps with them. For each one, ask who does it and what you do. Anything that lands on your side is work you are still doing.
  • Ask who writes, and whether it stays the same person. Voice depends on continuity, and on regular interviews rather than a single questionnaire.
  • Ask about AI. rallyup uses human writers and keeps AI to research. Whatever the answer, it should be clear.
  • Ask what cadence they hold. In our benchmark, the bigger gains came from consistency (a median 22 to 31 posts per 90 days) and breakout posts: the top 10% of posts produced 75% of impressions.
  • Ask for a sample report. Impressions alone are not enough. Look for profile views, ICP connections and inbound messages. As a reference point, the median engagement rate per post in our benchmark was 2.18% (453 posts).
  • Ask for results from someone like you. Named companies and real numbers, not only testimonials.
  • Check the terms. Minimum contract length, what happens if a post doesn't sound like you, and whether there is any guarantee.

when rallyup isn't the right fit

  • Your budget is under $5,000 a month per executive.
  • You want to write it yourself and just need editing.
  • You need meetings in the next 30 days rather than an audience that produces them over a quarter. An outbound-first agency fits better.
  • Your goals are B2C or creator growth. We write for B2B founders and executives selling to other businesses.
  • You can't give a 30-minute story interview twice a month. That conversation is where the posts come from.
