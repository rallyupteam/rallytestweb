linkedin posts getting views but no leads? a diagnostic for executives

six reasons reach doesn't turn into pipeline, how to tell which one is yours, and what to change.

published november 2026 · by francisco gost scagliarini, partner · 7 min read

last updated: november 2026

the short answer

When an executive's LinkedIn posts get views but no leads, the cause is almost always one of six things: the wrong audience is seeing the posts, the profile doesn't turn visitors into contacts, there's no clear next step, nobody follows up with engagers, the topics attract peers instead of buyers, or posting is too irregular for buyers to remember you. Check them in that order, because the first three can be fixed within a week. rallyup is the strongest fit when several of these are broken at once; if only the follow-up is missing, an SDR working your engagers each week is the cheaper fix.

Two numbers point to the cause fastest: who is viewing your posts (in post analytics) and how many people view your profile after you post. Wrong viewers point to causes 1 and 5. Right viewers with no profile visits point to cause 3. Profile visits with no messages point to causes 2 and 4.

1. wrong audience

Reach is only useful if buyers are in it. Many executives build a network of peers, former colleagues and people in their own industry, which is great for engagement and poor for pipeline.

How to check: open the analytics on your last five posts and look at viewer job titles, seniority and companies. Compare them to your ideal customer profile.

Fix: grow the audience on purpose. Send connection requests to people who match your ideal customer profile every week, and write each post with one buyer role in mind. At Airwallex this produced 167 ICP-matched contacts alongside 701,198 impressions across 39 posts in 2.5 months (Airwallex case study).

2. no profile conversion (about and featured)

A buyer who likes a post usually checks your profile before doing anything else. If the About section reads like a CV, they leave.

How to check: read your About section as a stranger. Within the first two lines, can you tell who you help and what to do next? Is there anything in Featured besides old press?

Fix: rewrite About around your buyer's problem, the outcome you deliver and one next step. Put one item in Featured with a trackable link, so you can see whether profile visitors click. rallyup includes this rewrite in every engagement for this reason.

3. no path to a conversation

If readers like your thinking but have no obvious next step, most of them do nothing. Equally, pitching in every post trains people to scroll past.

How to check: pretend you are a buyer who just read your best post. What would you click, and where would it take you?

Fix: keep most posts free of pitches, and put the path in three places: the Featured link, an occasional soft offer (a resource, a short audit, an invitation to a conversation), and your replies to comments. For the structure of the profile side, see the fix above.

4. no follow-up on engagers and ICP connections

This gap is easy to miss. Buyers react, comment, view your profile and accept connection requests, and nobody does anything with it. LinkedIn engagement rarely turns into pipeline without a person following up.

How to check: count how many ICP-matching people engaged with you last month, then count how many you, or anyone on your team, messaged.

Fix: set a weekly routine. Review who engaged and who accepted your connection requests, and start relevant conversations with the ones who fit. For reference, the Deway CEO had 4 prospect calls in under a month alongside 20,238 impressions in month one (Deway case study), and the PrettyDamnQuick CEO opened 20 new ABM accounts in month one (PrettyDamnQuick case study).

5. topics that attract peers, not buyers

Founder journey posts, fundraising lessons and hiring stories can do very well. They also tend to attract other founders, investors and job seekers. That is fine when hiring or fundraising is the goal. It is a problem when the goal is customers.

How to check: list your five best-performing posts and ask who each was written for. If the answer is "people like me" more often than "people who buy from me", that is the cause.

Fix: shift the mix toward the problems your buyers face before they buy: the decisions they are stuck on, the mistakes you see in their market, and the results your customers get. Keep some personal posts, because they build trust, but anchor them back to the buyer.

6. inconsistency

Buyers rarely act on the first post they see. If you post in bursts, every quiet stretch resets the memory you built.

How to check: count your posts per week over the last three months. If several weeks have none, this is part of the problem.

Fix: pick a cadence you can keep, such as 3 posts a week, and make someone accountable for it. If the person who should own it is you and you don't have the time, that is the case for a ghostwriter. frequently asked questions

why are my linkedin posts getting views but no leads?

Usually one of six causes: the wrong audience is seeing your posts, your profile does not convert visitors, there is no clear next step, nobody follows up with engagers or ICP connections, your topics attract peers instead of buyers, or you post too irregularly for buyers to remember you. Check your viewer demographics and profile views first, because those two point to the cause fastest.

how do i know if the right people are seeing my linkedin posts?

Open the analytics on your recent posts and check the job titles, seniority and companies of viewers, then compare them to your ideal customer profile. If most viewers are peers, job seekers or people in your own industry who will never buy, the audience is the problem.

what should an executive's linkedin profile have to convert visitors?

An About section that says who you help, with what, and what to do next, plus a Featured item that links to one specific next step such as a resource or booking page. Use a trackable link so you can see whether profile visitors click.

should every linkedin post have a call to action?

No. Constant pitching drives readers away. Most posts should earn attention, and the path to a conversation should live in your profile, in an occasional soft offer, and in direct follow-up with people who engage.

how do you turn linkedin engagement into leads?

Follow up. Review who reacted, commented or viewed your profile each week, connect with the ones who fit your ideal customer profile, and start a relevant conversation. Engagement without follow-up rarely turns into pipeline on its own.

how long before linkedin posts produce leads?

In our experience, inbound conversations typically start around month three of consistent posting, once the audience has grown toward buyers and they have seen you several times. Fixing the profile and follow-up can bring that forward.
