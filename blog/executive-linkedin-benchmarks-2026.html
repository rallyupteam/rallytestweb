executive linkedin benchmarks 2026

what happened to 14 b2b executives' linkedin reach, followers and engagement in their first 90 days of a ghostwriting program. every number with its sample size.

published november 2026 · by francisco gost scagliarini, partner · 9 min read

the short answer

Across 14 B2B executives on a done-for-you LinkedIn ghostwriting program in 2026, the median executive's monthly impressions grew 4.7x in the first 90 days compared with the 90 days before (n=7 executives with complete account data on both sides; 6 of 7 at least doubled). Followers grew a median 17.4% in about 90 days (n=9). The typical post only did a little better (median impressions per post rose 1.27x). The growth came from posting consistently and from breakout posts: 4.5% of posts passed 50,000 impressions, against none in the 90 days before.

rallyup publishes this report and ran the programs it measures, so read it as one agency's data, not an industry census. Every number below states its sample size, and no client is named.

1. monthly reach grew 4.7x

We compared each executive's total LinkedIn impressions in the 90 days before their first ghostwritten post with the 90 days after it. This is account-level data, so it counts everything the account earned, not only the posts we wrote. Seven executives had complete daily data on both sides of that line.

The median executive went from 27,640 to 88,243 impressions a month, a 4.7x increase. Six of the seven at least doubled. One went down (0.6x): that executive was already posting heavily before and published fewer posts during the program (37 in the 90 days before, 21 after). A second method, adding up post-level impressions for 9 executives, gives the same answer: 4.7x.

2. the typical post barely changes

The median post went from 1,376 impressions to 1,747, only 1.27x. If you judge a LinkedIn program by how a normal Tuesday post does, it looks like a small gain. That is the wrong unit.

Two things moved instead. Executives posted more consistently (a median of 22 posts in the 90 days before, 31 after), and a small number of posts broke out far beyond their usual audience.

After kickoff, the top 10% of posts produced 75% of all impressions, up from 48% before. Across all 14 executives, six posts passed 100,000 impressions. Nobody can schedule a breakout post. What a program changes is how many good attempts you take: more posts, each built on a real story or a clear point of view, and each one a chance to escape your existing network.

For an executive this means two practical things. Judge a program on monthly totals and on breakouts, not on the median post. And give it enough posts to find the outliers: at around two posts a week, that takes most of a quarter.

4. followers grow about 17% in 90 days

Across 9 executives with at least 80 days on the program, followers grew a median of 17.4% from kickoff to day 90. Across all 14 executives, measured from kickoff to November 2026, the median was 21.7%. Follower growth was steadier than impressions: every one of the 14 grew, from under 1% (two weeks in) to 50.9%.

5. starting from zero

Four executives had posted fewer than eight times before starting, so we left them out of the before-and-after averages: a ratio against almost nothing is meaningless. Two of them are still worth reporting. One reached 145,071 impressions in their first 71 days, and the other 407,799 in their first 56 days, after earning 0 and 414 impressions in the measured days before.

The other two had been on the program for less than a month when we pulled the data, which is too early to say anything.

6. what correlates with reach

These patterns use all 453 posts published after kickoff. To stop large accounts from dominating, each post is compared with its own author's median.

  • Comments matter more than likes. Posts in the top third for comments relative to reactions reached 1.16x their author's median; the bottom third reached 0.85x. Posts that start a conversation travel further.
  • Tuesday and Friday did best. Tuesday posts reached 1.15x the author's median (137 posts) and Friday 1.19x (34 posts). Wednesday was weakest at 0.86x (127 posts). Days are in UTC, across several time zones.
  • Format made no measurable difference. Images, video and text posts all landed at about 1.0x their author's median. 380 of the 453 posts carried an image and none were carousels, so this sample can't say much about carousels.

methodology and limits

  • Sample. 20 LinkedIn personal accounts of rallyup clients were reviewed. 14 executives at 11 B2B companies had at least five posts after kickoff and are included: 4 in AI, 4 in fintech, 1 in B2B SaaS, 1 in venture capital and 4 in other B2B sectors. No client is named, and figures are not attributable to a company.
  • Kickoff. The date of each executive's first post scheduled through the program. "Before" is the 90 days before that date; "after" is the 90 days from it, or until November 10, 2026 if sooner.
  • Data. LinkedIn's own analytics for each personal account, synced through Ordinal. Account-level totals cover all account activity. Post-level figures exclude reposts and posts with missing metrics (2 of 455).
  • Who is included. Account-level before-and-after uses only the 7 executives with 80 or more days of daily data on both sides. Follower growth at 90 days uses the 9 executives with 80 or more days on the program.
  • Limits. This is a small sample of people who chose to hire a ghostwriting agency, with no control group. It shows what happened, not what would have happened without a program. Medians are used throughout because a few very large posts skew averages.

when rallyup isn't the right fit

  • Your budget is under $5,000 a month per executive. A good freelance ghostwriter is the better choice.
  • You want to write your posts yourself and only need an editor.
  • You need booked meetings in the next 30 days more than an audience. An outbound-first agency fits better.
  • You're building a B2C or creator audience rather than B2B pipeline.
  • You can't give your writer a 30-minute story interview twice a month. Posts in your voice need your stories.
