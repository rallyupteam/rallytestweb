the short answer

Three kinds of service help founders book sales calls from LinkedIn: content-led ghostwriting agencies that build an audience of buyers, outbound agencies that run cold connection requests and DM sequences, and combined programs that pair executive content with ICP connection requests and follow-up. Outbound is the fastest way to a first meeting; content takes a quarter to build but compounds, because buyers already know you when the call starts. rallyup is the strongest fit for B2B founders and executives who want calls to come from their own posts and a growing network of ideal customers, with the writing and the connection requests done for them; if you need meetings in the next 30 days, an outbound-first agency fits better.

the three types of service

1. Content-led: linkedin ghostwriting agencies. A writer interviews the founder and publishes posts in their voice, usually several times a week. Calls come in when buyers who have read you for a while reach out, or when your team follows up with the people engaging. This builds trust and keeps working after a campaign ends, but on its own it waits for buyers to act. Some agencies in this group stop at the writing, so ask what happens after a post goes live. Our comparison of linkedin ghostwriting agencies covers the main options.

2. Outbound: lead generation and appointment-setting agencies. These run cold connection requests and DM sequences from the founder's account (or a sales rep's) to a targeted list, then hand over or book the replies. It is the quickest route to a first meeting and easy to measure. The trade-off is trust: a buyer who has never seen your name is reading a cold message, so reply rates depend heavily on targeting and copy. Some agencies here have added content, because, as SalesBread says of its own clients, accounts that post regularly see higher acceptance and reply rates. Windmill Growth sells a standalone LinkedIn Outbound plan at $999 a month, and Media Engine bundles outreach and appointment setting with ghostwriting.

3. Combined: executive content plus ICP connection programs. These pair a ghostwriting program with deliberate audience building: connection requests to your ideal customer profile every week, a profile that turns visitors into conversations, and lists of the buyers who engage so someone can follow up. rallyup works this way, and Windmill Growth's Founder OS plan ($3,999 a month) also bundles content with ICP outreach and inbox management. The idea is that the people you connect with see your posts for weeks before anyone asks for a call.

which one fits you

  • You need meetings this month. Start with outbound. It is the only one of the three built for speed.
  • Your buyers are senior and skeptical of cold messages. Content or a combined program. Senior buyers tend to check your profile and posts before they reply to anything.
  • You already run outbound in-house. Add content and audience building, not a second outbound engine. Your SDRs get warmer replies when prospects recognise the founder.
  • You want pipeline that keeps coming after the contract. Content or combined. The audience and the network you build stay with you.
  • You post already and get reach but no calls. The gap is usually the path, not the writing. Our diagnostic for linkedin views but no leads walks through the six usual causes.

the path from post to booked call

Whichever service you buy, calls come from the same five steps. If one is missing, posts turn into reach and nothing else.

  1. A profile that converts. An About section that says who you help and what to do next, and one trackable link in Featured. Buyers who like a post check the profile first.
  2. ICP connections every week. Send connection requests to people who match your ideal customer profile, so your posts reach buyers instead of only peers.
  3. An engagers list. Each week or month, pull the people who reacted, commented, viewed your profile or accepted a request, and filter for the ones who fit.
  4. DM follow-up. Someone, you or a sales rep, starts a relevant conversation with those people. Reference the post they engaged with. Don't pitch in the first message.
  5. A booking link. When the conversation turns to a call, send one link to your calendar. Put the same link in Featured so warm readers can book without asking.

Content agencies usually own step 1 and the posts. Outbound agencies own steps 2 and 4 with cold lists. A combined program covers steps 1 to 3 and hands step 4 to your team, or runs it with you.

how to vet a provider

  • Ask for calls booked, not impressions. Impressions are easy to grow and say little about pipeline. Ask how many calls or meetings their clients booked, over what period, and how they counted them.
  • Ask who does the follow-up. If the answer is "you", make sure you have the time or a rep who will.
  • Ask how they pick who to connect with. Good targeting is a written ideal customer profile by role, seniority and company, not a broad keyword search.
  • For outbound, ask about volume and account safety. High cold volume from a founder's own account can strain both the account and the brand.
  • For content, ask who writes and how they get material. Regular interviews with one dedicated writer produce posts that sound like you.
  • Ask for a sample report. Look for ICP connections, engagers, inbound DMs and calls, not only reach.

when rallyup isn't the right fit

  • You want a team to send cold DMs at volume and book calls into your calendar for you.
  • Your budget is under $5,000 a month per executive.
  • No one on your side will follow up with the buyers who engage. Content builds the warm list; someone still has to start the conversation.
