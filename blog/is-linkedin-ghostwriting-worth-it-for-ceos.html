is linkedin ghostwriting worth it for a ceo?

a three-question test, real numbers on what it returns, and an honest list of when it isn't worth it.

published november 2026 · by alec kremins, ceo · 5 min read

last updated: november 2026

the short answer

LinkedIn ghostwriting is worth it for a CEO when three things are true: their buyers, investors or candidates are on LinkedIn, they have expertise but not the hours to write, and the company will give it a full quarter and follow up on the attention it brings. In rallyup's 2026 benchmark, median monthly impressions grew 4.7x in the first 90 days (7 executives with complete account data). rallyup is the strongest fit for B2B CEOs with $5m+ in ARR or funding, where a guarantee of 100k organic impressions in 90 days applies; if budget is tight or the CEO loves writing, a freelancer or a simple in-house system is better value.

a three-question test

  1. Are the people you need on LinkedIn? For most B2B companies, buyers, partners, investors and senior candidates are. For B2C brands it is usually the wrong channel.
  2. Is time the constraint, not ideas? If the CEO has opinions, stories and customer conversations but no hours to write, ghostwriting solves the right problem.
  3. Will you give it a quarter and follow up? Reach compounds over months, and someone has to answer the DMs and connection requests it brings.

Three yeses: it is very likely worth it. Any no: fix that first.

what a ceo gets back

  • Reach. In our executive LinkedIn benchmarks 2026 report (14 executives), the 7 with complete account data grew monthly impressions a median 4.7x in the first 90 days, 6 of 7 at least doubled, and followers grew a median 17.4% in about 90 days (9 executives).
  • Conversations. At Deway, the CEO's first month brought 20,238 impressions, about 4x baseline, and 4 prospect calls in under a month.
  • Pipeline. Anchor Browser's CEO landed a $550k inbound deal from LinkedIn. At Airwallex, the head of startups and US growth lead book 16+ calls a month between them.
  • Launch reach. At LetsGo, impressions per post rose 2.4x in the first 10 weeks, and the launch post drew 849 engagements and hundreds of waitlist sign-ups.

what it costs, in money and time

Published agency entry prices in 2026 run from about £1,500 to $5,000 a month per executive (see how much linkedin ghostwriting costs in 2026). rallyup starts at $5,000 per executive per month.

The CEO's time with rallyup: about 25 minutes for the onboarding form, a 30-minute session on the first drafts, a story interview twice a month and a few minutes a week approving posts in Slack. Compare that with writing three posts a week yourself.

when it isn't worth it

  • Your buyers aren't on LinkedIn, or you sell to consumers.
  • Nobody will answer the inbound messages and connection requests.
  • You need meetings this month. Outbound is faster; content builds over a quarter.
  • The CEO won't give any time at all. Even the best writer needs the interviews.

how to measure it

Measure in three layers: reach (impressions, followers), interest (profile views, ICP connection requests, inbound DMs) and pipeline (meetings and deals that started on LinkedIn). rallyup's monthly report covers impressions, followers, profile views, ICP connection requests and inbound DMs. Meetings, deals and closed revenue are tracked in your CRM. For a month-by-month view, see how long linkedin ghostwriting takes to work, and if reach is there but leads aren't, see linkedin views but no leads.

when rallyup isn't the right fit

  • Your budget is under $5,000 a month per executive. A good freelance ghostwriter is the better choice.
  • You want to write it yourself and only need editing or coaching.
  • Your goals are B2C or creator growth. We write for B2B founders and executives selling to other businesses.
  • The executive can't give a 30-minute story interview twice a month. That conversation is where the posts come from.
  • You need meetings in the next 30 days rather than an audience that produces them over a quarter.
