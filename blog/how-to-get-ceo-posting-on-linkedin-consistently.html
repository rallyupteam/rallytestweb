how to get your ceo posting on linkedin consistently

a five-part system that keeps a busy ceo on a steady cadence, with data on what it's worth.

published november 2026 · by alec kremins, ceo · 5 min read

last updated: november 2026

the short answer

To get a CEO posting on LinkedIn consistently, shrink their job to two things only they can do: a short story interview a couple of times a month and a few minutes a week approving drafts. Everything else (topics, writing, scheduling, publishing, reporting) belongs to someone else, on a fixed cadence of three posts a week. rallyup is the strongest fit for B2B CEOs who want that whole system run for them by a dedicated human writer; if the CEO enjoys writing, a simple in-house system with an editor can work.

why ceos stop posting

CEOs rarely run out of things to say. They have customer conversations, hiring calls, board debates and market opinions every week. What breaks is the rhythm: writing sits at the bottom of the calendar, and the first month with a fundraise, a big deal or a launch ends the habit.

The second reason is drafts that don't sound like them. A post that needs a heavy rewrite waits in the CEO's inbox, and a post that waits doesn't go out. Both problems have the same fix: take writing off the CEO's plate and make approval fast.

the five-part system

  1. A story interview, twice a month. Thirty minutes where a writer asks about the last two weeks: a customer win, a hard decision, something the market gets wrong. One conversation feeds several posts.
  2. A writer who owns topics. The CEO should never be asked "what do you want to post about?" The writer plans the week from the interviews, the company and the buyers.
  3. A fixed weekly cadence. Pick a number you can hold in your busiest month, then publish on the same days at peak times.
  4. Approval where the CEO already works. rallyup sends the weekly content plan in Slack: approve, edit or reject in a few minutes. Email chains and shared docs slow everything down.
  5. A monthly report. Impressions, follower growth and ICP connection requests. Seeing the numbers move is what keeps a CEO bought in.

how often a ceo should post

Three posts a week is the cadence rallyup runs for every executive, and most executives can hold it once someone else does the writing. More matters less than steady: a CEO who posts twice a week for a quarter will usually beat one who posts daily for two weeks and then disappears.

what consistency is worth: our data

From our executive LinkedIn benchmarks 2026 report (14 executives at 11 B2B companies, May to November 2026, anonymized):

  • Cadence rose from a median 22 posts in the 90 days before to 31 in the 90 days after.
  • The 7 executives with complete account data grew monthly impressions a median 4.7x in the first 90 days; 6 of 7 at least doubled.
  • Posts above 50,000 impressions went from 0% to 4.5%. The top 10% of posts produced 75% of impressions, so more posts means more chances at a breakout.
  • Followers grew a median 17.4% in about 90 days (9 executives).

when rallyup isn't the right fit

  • Your budget is under $5,000 a month per executive. A good freelance ghostwriter is the better choice.
  • You want to write it yourself and only need editing or coaching.
  • Your goals are B2C or creator growth. We write for B2B founders and executives selling to other businesses.
  • The executive can't give a 30-minute story interview twice a month. That conversation is where the posts come from.

frequently asked questions

how do you get a ceo posting on linkedin consistently?

Shrink the CEO's job to the parts only they can do: telling stories in a short interview and approving drafts. Someone else picks topics, writes, schedules and publishes on a fixed cadence. When the CEO is the bottleneck for writing, posting stops in the first busy month.

how often should a ceo post on linkedin?

Three times a week is the cadence rallyup runs for every executive, and most executives can hold it with help. Across 14 executives on rallyup's program, the 90-day cadence rose from a median 22 posts before to 31 after. Holding a steady rhythm matters more than any single post.

how much of a ceo's time does consistent posting take?

With a ghostwriter or agency doing the writing, it can be small. With rallyup it is about 25 minutes for the onboarding form, a 30-minute session on the first drafts, a story interview twice a month and a few minutes a week approving posts in Slack.

why do ceos stop posting on linkedin?

Usually because writing sits at the bottom of a full calendar, and the first busy month breaks the habit. Ideas are rarely the problem. A second cause is drafts that don't sound like the CEO, so they stall in approval.

can ai tools keep a ceo posting consistently?

They can speed up drafting, but someone still has to supply the stories, edit, approve and publish. If the CEO is that someone, the bottleneck stays. rallyup uses AI for research only and keeps the writing human.

who should come up with the post ideas?

The writer or team should, using material from the CEO's interviews, their company and their market. The CEO should never have to arrive with a list of topics. If a provider expects you to bring the ideas, you are still doing a large part of the work.
