the four agency types

"Social presence" covers very different jobs. A CEO who wants buyers to know their name, a founder who wants a Forbes feature and a marketing team that wants the company page posting daily are buying different things, even if they all say "presence". Most agencies are built for one of these jobs and add the others on top.

which type fits which goal

  • Pipeline and inbound. If you want buyers to start conversations with you, the work is a steady cadence of useful posts, a profile that converts and connections with the right people. An executive LinkedIn specialist is built for this.
  • Press and credibility. If the goal is coverage, bylines and speaking slots, a PR-led firm fits. Some, such as CSuite Content, combine PR with LinkedIn posts.
  • Brand awareness across channels. If the company page, paid social and several networks all need managing, a broad social or content agency fits, with executive posting as one piece.
  • Hiring and employer brand. Candidates look up the founder before they apply. A specialist keeps the executive's feed active and on message; a workshop-based consultancy suits a larger team learning to post themselves.
  • An executive who wants to write. If you enjoy writing and want a method, a personal-branding consultancy or coach costs you less than paying someone to write for you.

Many companies want two of these at once. That's fine, but pick the one that matters most this year and hire for it. An agency that does a little of everything rarely does the main job as well as a specialist.

why linkedin carries most b2b executive presence

For B2B executives, the people who matter (buyers, partners, investors and senior hires) are on LinkedIn in a work frame of mind, and they look at the executive's profile, not just the company page. That is why we treat executive presence and LinkedIn as close to the same thing for B2B, and why every program we run is LinkedIn first.

It also changes what you should buy. On LinkedIn, the person posting is the channel. Posts from an executive's own account, in their own voice, reach people a brand page doesn't. Other channels still matter for some goals, press and podcasts above all, but most B2B leaders get more from doing LinkedIn well than from being thinly present everywhere.

how to measure executive presence

Presence is easy to claim and hard to see, so agree on numbers before you start. These are the ones we track, with what the executives in our executive linkedin benchmarks 2026 report achieved (14 B2B executives at 11 companies, May to November 2026, anonymized).

MeasureWhat it tells youBenchmark from our report
Monthly impressionsHow many people see the executive at allMedian 4.7x in the first 90 days vs the 90 days before (n=7 executives with complete data; 6 of 7 at least doubled)
Follower growthWhether the audience is compoundingMedian +17.4% in about 90 days (n=9 executives)
Engagement rate per postWhether the right people react and replyMedian 2.18% (n=453 posts)
CadenceWhether the executive actually shows upMedian 22 to 31 posts per 90 days, before vs after
Breakout postsWhether some posts travel well beyond the networkPosts above 50,000 impressions: 0% before, 4.5% after

Reach numbers are the start, not the finish. For pipeline, add profile views, ICP connection requests accepted and inbound DMs; for press, count coverage and invitations; for hiring, ask candidates where they first saw you. If an agency reports only impressions, ask what else it can show. For the business side, see linkedin posts getting views but no leads.

when rallyup isn't the right fit

  • You need press coverage, bylines or speaking slots. A PR-led firm is built for that.
  • You want one agency to manage the company page and several social networks. A broad social media agency fits better.
  • You want to write your own posts and need coaching or editing. A personal-branding consultancy or an editor costs less.
  • Your budget is under $5,000 a month per executive.
  • Your goals are B2C or creator growth. We write for B2B founders and executives selling to other businesses.
  • You can't give a 30-minute story interview twice a month. That conversation is where the posts come from.

frequently asked questions

what are the best agencies for b2b executive social presence?

It depends on the goal. For pipeline and inbound, executive LinkedIn specialists such as rallyup, Windmill Growth and Kurogo fit best; rallyup is the strongest fit for B2B founders and leadership teams who want a dedicated writer per executive. For press, a PR-led firm such as CSuite Content. For multi-channel and paid social, broader agencies such as Sculpt, Understory or storyarb. For coaching an executive who writes their own posts, consultancies such as Klowt or SimplyBe.

should a b2b executive hire a pr firm or a linkedin ghostwriting agency?

Hire a PR firm if the goal is earned media, bylines and speaking slots. Hire a LinkedIn ghostwriting agency if the goal is a steady presence in front of buyers, partners and hires, and conversations that lead to pipeline. Some agencies, such as CSuite Content, combine PR with LinkedIn posts, and Kurogo offers PR alongside ghostwriting.

is a social media agency a good choice for executive presence?

It can be if you need the company page, paid social and several networks managed together, with executive posting as one part of the program. If the executive's own LinkedIn presence is the main goal, a specialist that writes in the executive's voice and interviews them regularly is usually the better buy.

why focus executive social presence on linkedin?

For B2B executives, buyers, partners, investors and senior hires are on LinkedIn and look at the executive's own profile. Posts from an executive's account reach people a brand page doesn't. Other channels matter for some goals, but most B2B leaders get more from doing LinkedIn well than from a thin presence everywhere.

how do you measure b2b executive social presence?

Track monthly impressions, follower growth, engagement rate per post and cadence, then add profile views, ICP connections and inbound messages. In rallyup's executive LinkedIn benchmarks 2026, the median executive's monthly impressions grew 4.7x in the first 90 days (n=7), followers grew a median 17.4% in about 90 days (n=9) and the median engagement rate per post was 2.18% (n=453 posts).
