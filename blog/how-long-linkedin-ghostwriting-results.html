week 0 to 1: setup

The first week is about getting enough of you onto the page that a writer can sound like you. At rallyup that means a 25-minute onboarding form, then a 30-minute alignment session on your first drafts. Early on we also rewrite your About section and add a trackable link to Featured, so the profile is ready for the people your posts send to it.

Your first post is usually live about a week after kickoff. From then on the weekly content plan arrives in Slack and you approve, edit or reject each post before it goes out.

What to measure: nothing yet. Write down your baseline instead: average impressions per post over the last few months, follower count, and how many inbound messages you got from buyers. Every later number needs this to mean anything.

month 1: calibration

Month one is when the writer learns which of your stories and opinions land with your audience. Some posts will outperform, some won't, and both teach the writer something. Your first story interview happens here, and from then on it runs twice a month.

Calibration months can still produce real results. From our case studies:

  • A PandaDoc executive's first post reached 27,000+ impressions (PandaDoc case study).
  • The Deway CEO reached 20,238 impressions in month one, about 4x baseline, and had 4 prospect calls in under a month (Deway case study).
  • The PrettyDamnQuick CEO opened 20 new ABM accounts in month one (PrettyDamnQuick case study).

These are among our strongest starts, which is why they are published. Treat them as what is possible in month one, not as a promise for every executive.

What to measure: impressions and engagement against your baseline, and which topics drew comments from people in your ideal customer profile.

month 2: compounding

By month two the topics are sharper, the posting rhythm is steady and the audience is growing in the right direction, partly through automated ICP connection requests. This is where growth starts to compound: each post reaches more people because the previous ones grew the audience.

The clearest example in our case studies is a VC partner at Lightbank, who went from 32 impressions across two months before working with us to 405,397 in the next two (September 8 to November 9, 2026), with followers up 33% (Lightbank case study).

What to measure: follower growth, profile views and ICP connection requests. If impressions are rising but profile views are flat, your profile or your topics need work. Our diagnostic for views but no leads covers how to tell which.

month 3: conversations

Month three is when an audience starts behaving like a pipeline source. Buyers who have seen several of your posts begin to reply, accept connection requests and take calls. This is also the end of rallyup's 90-day guarantee window for 100k organic impressions.

Our largest published example is Airwallex, where a head of startups and a US growth lead reached 701,198 impressions across 39 posts in 2.5 months, gained 2,496 followers, built 167 ICP-matched contacts with 16+ calls booked a month (Airwallex case study).

What to measure: inbound DMs, prospect calls, accounts opened and deals your sales team can trace to LinkedIn. Your monthly progress report should show these alongside reach.

how these numbers compare across clients

The case studies above are individual results. Across 14 executives in our executive LinkedIn benchmarks 2026, the median executive's monthly impressions grew 4.7x in the first 90 days (7 executives with complete data on both sides) and followers grew a median 17.4% (9 executives). The typical post improved 1.27x, and the bigger gains came from consistency and a handful of breakout posts.

what speeds it up

You can't skip the calendar, but you can make each month count for more:

  • Bring specifics to interviews. A deal you lost, a number that surprised you, a customer conversation from this week. Specific stories make the posts people remember.
  • Reply to buyers yourself. When someone in your ideal customer profile comments, a reply from you starts the relationship your posts opened.
  • Follow up on new ICP connections. Every accepted request is a person who chose to see your posts. A short, relevant message turns some of them into conversations.

what slows it down

  • Skipped interviews. Without fresh stories, posts drift toward generic opinions and engagement drops.
  • Slow approvals. A post waiting a week for sign-off breaks the rhythm. A few minutes a week in Slack is enough.
  • A profile that doesn't convert. Readers who click through to a vague About section leave.
  • Posting for peers. Topics that impress other founders can grow followers without growing pipeline.
  • Stopping at day 45. Judging the program halfway through month two cuts it off before compounding has had time to show.

when rallyup isn't the right fit

  • You need meetings in the next 30 days rather than an audience. Outbound or paid campaigns are faster.
  • You can't commit to 90 days. Shorter tests rarely show what a program can do.
  • Your budget is under $5,000 a month per executive.
  • You want to write it yourself and only need editing.
  • You can't give a 30-minute story interview twice a month.
